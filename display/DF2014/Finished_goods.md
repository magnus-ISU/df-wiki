**Finished goods** are items that go to a finished goods [stockpile](Stockpile.md) when made. Many of these items are frequently referred to as trade goods as they are lightweight and suitably valuable for [trading](Trading.md). They include **crafts**, **goblets**, **instruments**, **toys**, **large gems**, and **totems**, some of which have several sub-types.

Finished good types with several sub-types may have generic workshop orders to produce random sub-type items, e.g. "Make crafts". Additionally, some specific subtypes of goods can be ordered directly at an appropriate workshop or through the [manager](Manager.md). If you have a mandate to make rings, you can order rings directly, instead of waiting for a generic "crafts" job to produce rings; others, like puzzleboxes, though, can only be produced by generic jobs ("toys").

Some types of trade goods (Crafts and Goblets) can be produced in multiples. It is possible to get up to three crafts from a single resource. Skill does not affect the chance to produce multiples.

### Types of trade goods

#### Crafts

Crafts include [**figurines**](Figurine.md), [**rings**, **earrings**, **amulets**, **bracelets**, **crowns**](Jewelry.md), and **[scepters](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php?title=DF2014:Scepter&action=edit&redlink=1)**. They are the only type of trade good that appears on its own page in the Trade Depot menu. Some subtypes of crafts can reference [historical figures](Historical_figure.md) and events (figurines, for example); this does not have any meaningful effect on the value of the craft.

One generic order to "make crafts" consumes one resource and produces one to three items, two on average. All subtypes, if they can be produced from the chosen material, have an identical chance to be produced.

Most crafts can be worn by dwarves like clothing (figurines and scepters can only be carried in hands). Dwarves ordered to haul a craft will occasionally claim it instead (either putting it on or storing it in a room), satisfying the "acquire something" [need](Need.md); this is much more likely to happen if the dwarf has [preference](Preference.md) for the item.

Gem and glass crafts cannot be made directly; they may only be produced randomly by gem cutting jobs.

#### Goblets

`¶`

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e1/BLW_Betrothal_Goblet.jpg/250px-BLW_Betrothal_Goblet.jpg?utm_source=commons.wikimedia.org&utm_campaign=imageinfo&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:BLW_Betrothal_Goblet.jpg)

[https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:BLW_Betrothal_Goblet.jpg](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:BLW_Betrothal_Goblet.jpg)

A goblet.

Goblets have no subtypes, but have different names depending on the material from which they are made: [metal](Metal.md) and [glass](Glass.md) goblets are simply **goblets**, [stone](Stone.md) goblets are called **mugs**, and [wooden](Wood.md) goblets are called **cups**.

Goblets are always produced at a 3:1 ratio. Metal goblets are made at a [metalsmith's forge](Metalsmith's_forge.md). Glass goblets are made at a [glass furnace](Glass_furnace.md). Rock mugs and wooden cups are made at a [craftsdwarf's workshop](Craftsdwarf's_workshop.md).

Goblets can be used by dwarves for [drinking](Drink.md)<sup>[v0.42.01](../Category/Version_0.42.01.md)</sup>. If no goblets are available in a dining room stockpile or a coffer in a [tavern](Tavern.md), a thirsty dwarf can drink [alcohol](Alcohol.md) straight from the [barrel](Barrel.md), but this will result in an unhappy [thought](Thought.md). If a dwarf is unable to use their arms, they will stall on picking up a goblet rather than drinking straight from the barrel<sup>[Bug](#bugs):[9440](https://dwarffortressbugtracker.com/view.php?id=9440)</sup>. Goblets can also be used to transport liquids in [adventurer mode](Adventurer_mode.md).

#### Instruments

> *Main article: [Instruments](Instruments.md)*

[![](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/thumb/6/60/Wood_inst.jpg/150px-Wood_inst.jpg)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Wood_inst.jpg)

[https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Wood_inst.jpg](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Wood_inst.jpg)

Wooden instruments.

`¿` Instruments can be composed of one or more components made from specific materials. The presence of multiple high-value components can increase the trade value of an instrument far beyond any other finished good. Instruments can also be used by [performers](Performer.md) in [taverns](Tavern.md) or [temples](Temple.md).

#### Toys

`æ` Toys include **mini-forges**, **toy hammers**, **toy axes**, **toy boats**, and **puzzleboxes**. Dwarven children can play with toys.

#### Totems

[![](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/thumb/c/c2/Skull_totem.jpg/200px-Skull_totem.jpg)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Skull_totem.jpg)

[https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Skull_totem.jpg](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Skull_totem.jpg)

An example of a skull totem.

`ç` Totems are made from skulls by a [bone carver](Bone_carver.md) at a [craftsdwarf's workshop](Craftsdwarf's_workshop.md).

Although totems have the same base [value](Value.md) as all other crafts, totems made from the skulls of more valuable animals like [unicorns](Unicorn.md) will fetch a hefty price.

#### Large gems

`♦` Large gems have no subtypes. Large gems can be ordered cut from every material **except** gems and glass (including beeswax and soap). For [gem](Gem.md) or [glass](Glass.md) large gems, a Gem Cutting job will occasionally produce a large gem. It can be assumed that this represents the rarity of large gem-quality rough gemstones, and the difficulty of creating thick evenly structured glass pieces. Large gems currently have no use beyond trade.

### Materials

Most materials can be used to make trade goods - on occasion, a [dwarf](Dwarf.md) in a [strange mood](Strange_mood.md) will make a trade good out of a material not normally suited for it (e. g., a bone toy).
