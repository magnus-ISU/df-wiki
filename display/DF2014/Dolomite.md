**Dolomite** is a [white](Color.md) medium-[value](Value.md) and economic type of [sedimentary](Sedimentary_layer.md) [stone](Stone.md) that forms entire layers. It is also a [flux](Flux.md) stone, meaning it can be used in the production of [pig iron](Pig_iron.md) and [steel](Steel.md). Additionally, it is [magma safe](Magma_safe.md), with an extremely high melting point of 16507 °U.

In fact, dolomite has the highest [melting point](Temperature.md#melting-point) of all common\* stones and metals. It is not safe from the scorching 50000 °U heat of [dragon](Dragon.md) fire, though it will last longer than most minerals.

(\* The uncommon exceptions being [raw adamantine](Raw_adamantine.md) ore, [adamantine](Adamantine.md) metal, and, because it cannot be melted, [slade](Slade.md).)

Because of its abundance and utility, dolomite is one of the most remarkable non-[ore](Ore.md) stones, and is much more useful than many other flux stones.

Dolomite is slightly denser than the average rock, inhibiting [hauling](Hauling.md). Because of its status as a flux stone, which are in high demand in [steel](Steel.md)-making, [wheelbarrows](Wheelbarrow.md) are useful for the transport of dolomite.

### In real life

Dolomite is comprised of calcium magnesium carbonate (CaMg(CO<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>) and is related to [limestone](Limestone.md), [chalk](Chalk.md), and [marble](Marble.md). Its ionic and crystalline nature contributes to its high melting point. It's dolomite, baby!

However Iron men are known to frequently make illogical and exaggerated claims about their composition, especially ones whose internal mechanisms are powered by alcohol. As dolomite is magma-safe, it is sometimes touted that it "won't cop out when the heat's all about".
