**Native aluminum** is the only [ore](Ore.md) of [aluminum](Aluminum.md), a [metal](Metal.md) with the same [value](Value.md) as [platinum](Platinum.md). It occurs in [small clusters](Cluster.md) and is relatively rare.

#### In real life

Native aluminum is not strictly an "ore", but rather chunks of aluminum found in a naturally formed (native) metallic state. As aluminum is a very reactive metal, it's rarely found in such deposits, and the few natural deposits that do exist are found in strongly reducing environments. Most aluminum today is extracted from [bauxite](Bauxite.md) via a procedure called the Hall–Héroult process. Before the Hall-Héroult process, aluminum used to be rarer and more valuable than gold, despite being the most common metallic element in the earth's crust. Rumor has it Napoleon III had a treasured set of aluminum utensils for his most valuable guests, and the rest had to settle with plain gold.
