[Dwarves](Dwarf.md) in [strange moods](Strange_mood.md) will create **legendary artifacts**: unique, "named" [items](Item.md) which are of unsurpassable [quality](Item_quality.md) (and often, [value](Value.md) as well). An artifact is the ultimate expression of a dwarf's desires, fears, memories and hopes in art-form, and each dwarf will produce at most one in their lives (or [die](Insanity.md) trying). Dwarves that create an artifact immediately gain enough [experience](Experience.md) to boost them to [legendary](Legendary.md) level in the affected [skill](Strange_mood.md#skills-and-workshops), unless they were [possessed](Strange_mood.md#possessed).

Dwarves drop artifacts in the [workshop](Workshop.md) as soon as they are made. They can be traded, just like any item of their type, or stolen, sold or warred over, and may transfer between civilizations in those ways. A list of all artifacts in the fortress can be seen by pressing L (l in version 0.40).

### Uses

[![](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/thumb/6/65/Artifact_list.png/450px-Artifact_list.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Artifact_list.png)

[https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Artifact_list.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Artifact_list.png)

Small list of artifacts.

Artifacts are normally of extremely high [value](Value.md) and [quality](Quality.md), and can be used just like normal items of their type; they're therefore extremely useful in any situation where value or quality are important. Note that material and weight calculations still apply, so an artifact sword made of dingo bone, or an artifact breastplate made of gold, are not going to be overwhelmingly powerful.

[https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Holy_Grail.jpg](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Holy_Grail.jpg)

The holy grail, one of the most well-known, symbolic artifacts in the world. Unfortunately not made by dwarves. [On the other hand...](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Ring_des_Nibelungen)

#### Furniture

Artifact [doors](Door.md) and [hatches](Hatch.md) provide indestructible, instantly-lockable gates.

Artifact [furniture](Furniture.md) of high value can be placed in [rooms](Room.md) to greatly increase the room's value (and hence quality), which can be useful to meet the room quality requirements of [nobles](Noble.md). They can also be placed in high-traffic areas of the fort, so that dwarves that pass by (or over) them will get happy [thoughts](Thought.md) from admiring them. Artifact [mechanisms](Mechanism.md) can be turned into [levers](Lever.md) and [traps](Trap.md), which count as furniture.

Certain artifact types (barrels, buckets, mechanisms, etc.) can be used as components in some buildings; doing so will multiply the artifact's effect on fortress value. They can also potentially be displayed on a [pedestal](Pedestal.md) or in a [display case](Display_case.md).

#### Weapons/Armor

Artifact equipment get a large ×3 quality bonus to their weapon accuracy and armor deflection values (compared to ×2 to masterwork); this is not enough to make totally inappropriate materials competitive with [steel](Steel.md) masterwork equipment. Also note that while they will be half again more accurate, artifact blades do not have a sharper edge than masterwork items of their material; in other words, their material type modifiers remain unchanged, and they will do no more damage than a regular item of their type (though they will never dull.) (And currently get half the sharpness.<sup>[Bug](#bugs):[9549](https://dwarffortressbugtracker.com/view.php?id=9549)</sup>)

Artifact [weapons](Weapon.md) (if not being used in the military) can be put into a [weapon trap](Trap.md#weapon-trap), which will make it count as valuable [furniture](Furniture.md).

#### Finished Goods

[![](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/c/c5/Artifact_Storage.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Artifact_Storage.png)

[https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Artifact_Storage.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Artifact_Storage.png)

Finished goods are not particularly useful, only adding to your [wealth](../Main/Wealth.md) (and all of the things that entails). This bin is clutter for the fortress, valuable only for trading, but any and every thief's dream score, an object of worship for three different [kobold](Kobold.md) [civilizations](Civilization.md).

An artifact [gem](Gem.md) is called a perfect gem and functions the same as a large gem, having all the same uses, i.e., none, other than boosting fortress value and trading. [Display cases](Display_case.md) and [pedestals](Pedestal.md) can also be used to show off otherwise-useless artifact crafts.

#### Cages

Artifact cages and animal traps are unique, in that they are capable of containing **any** type of vermin creature without them ever escaping - by comparison, certain types of vermin can gnaw, fly, or immolate their way out of ordinary cages depending on their materials.

Any other art (including other artifacts) that references an artifact cage will mention what animals are currently in it - the artifact quality of the cage makes it capable of magically self-updating in all references to it in the world.

### Non-player artifacts

Artifacts can be created in [world generation](World_generation.md) and [afterwards](World_activities.md):

- Non-player dwarves can also be taken by **strange moods** and create artifacts of their own, except for furniture.
- All original **[books](Book.md)**, regardless of form or content, are considered artifacts - copies are not, however.
- Some heroic events in world generation will trigger the naming of an object. For instance, a warrior may [name](Name.md) a piece of equipment that they were carrying when they slew a [megabeast](Megabeast.md). This can be any kind of gear, from the weapon they used to kill it, to a low boot they were wearing (that, presumably, proved critical in combat). Once named, these **heroic objects** are also considered artifacts, though their quality and properties remain unchanged. Assuming the person who named it didn't die, they will still be in possession of it.
- Some artifacts qualify as **holy relics**; generated from a body part or clothing item that belonged to a [religion](Religion.md)'s dead high priest. These are usually stored in a [temple](Temple.md), separately from the remainder of the corpse, and displayed on a [pedestal](Pedestal.md) or in a [display case](Display_case.md).
- Some creatures will be blessed with [necromancy](Necromancy.md) through an artifact slab gifted by a [deity](Deity.md).
- [Certain spoilerous locations](Vault.md) contain a different kind of artifact slab.

Once created, artifacts are subject to various [claims](Claim.md) that depend on the circumstances surrounding their creation: if it's a single person (as opposed to an entity), the creator can **keep** it for themselves, **gift** it to their parent entity, or claim it as a **family heirloom**. This can be shown in Fortress mode, where the artifact creation announcement will also include the nature of the claim: "He/She offers it to \<entity\>", "He/she claims it as a family heirloom"/"He/she claims it as a family heirloom in the name of the family ancestor \<figure\>" or "He/she claims it as a personal treasure". On the other hand, if an entity was responsible for its creation, the entity will keep its claim on it (e.g. a [religion](Religion.md) and its priests over a holy relic, or a [library](Library.md) and its [scholars](Scholar.md) over a book).

[![](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/thumb/3/3e/ArtifactHeirloomExample.png/300px-ArtifactHeirloomExample.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:ArtifactHeirloomExample.png)

[https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:ArtifactHeirloomExample.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:ArtifactHeirloomExample.png)

Example of a dwarf claiming an artifact as an heirloom.

Regardless of their nature or classification, artifacts will then be subjected to the turmoil of the world:

- The bearer can be killed in combat, by enemies from another [civilization](Civilization.md) or a [megabeast](Megabeast.md). Often, the slayer will loot the artifact for themselves and keep it, [claim](Claim.md) it as a family heirloom themselves, or return it to their parent entity.
- More peaceful artifact transfers also happen for diplomacy purposes.
- Artifacts claimed as family heirlooms are passed down to descendants, if the bearer dies of old age. Those initial couples all get associated family structures, and then those links are passed along to the children and so forth. The descendant is chosen according to "importance", or otherwise at random.
- If stored at a [site](Site.md), the artifact can be stolen by creatures prone to thieving, like [kobolds](Kobold.md). If the site becomes invaded, the artifact can likewise be robbed by an invader.
- If a family or entity becomes deprived of an artifact they had laid a claim on, they may send representatives to claim it back: these come in the form of [questers](Quester.md) or [agents](Agent.md) roaming the world to gather information in more or less covert ways. Depending on the nature of the entities involved, they may approach the matter in different ways: a civilization may go to [war](War.md) if they find out the artifact resides in another civilization's site, or they may send mercenaries to kill a beast that stole it.
- Sometimes, artifacts can be "lost in the wilds". The circumstances surrounding such events are unclear, but this means they're effectively lost, for all intents and purposes: foreign questers will wander the world fruitlessly in search of it (unless they happen to stumble upon the artifact's exact spot in the entire world, which is extraordinarily unlikely), and squads you [send to retrieve](Mission.md) it will never return due to a bug.

As a player, you may interact with these artifacts in (currently two) different ways:

#### Fortress mode

You may send [squads](Squad.md) of dwarves on a [mission](Mission.md) to "retrieve" an artifact from a site. (If the artifact wasn't created by your fortress, it's not so much "retrieval" as "theft".) This includes sites residing outside civilizations, such as [necromancer](Necromancer.md) [towers](Tower.md) containing books of interest. If the artifact's location is not known, you may send your squads off to gather information on its whereabouts.

Note that the site's entity will keep its claim on the artifact you've stolen:

- If you're caught, expect a retaliatory invasion. If you've raided a site from a civilization you were at peace with, what you made was a declaration of [war](War.md).
- If you're not caught, you may still receive visits from [agents](Agent.md) and [questers](Quester.md) inquiring about the artifact's location. Once they know it's in your fortress, they may demand you bring it back, or sneak back with the intent to come back with an army to make their demands more convincing.

Under the rumour system, artifacts created in your fortress can also become known to foreign sites and civilisations. Some of their [historical figures](Historical_figure.md) may then lay claim to your artifacts, and plot to steal them. Artifacts that are built within your fortress (chests, animal traps, mechanisms and the like) cannot be stolen.

If an artifact was [on display](Display_case.md) you will be notified of the theft, either someone saw it being taken, or if there are no witnesses you get: "X is missing from its proper place!" In this case, all might not be lost. In order to retrieve it,

1. First check if it is still on the map. If the artifact list (L) does not show it anymore then it is gone.
2. If it is still there, it will be hidden in someone's clothing. The theft (invariably?) involves one of your own dwarves and one visitor, the dwarf stealing it and the visitor obtaining it from them. You would have to manually check (v-i, scroll down below hauled items) 200+ of your dwarves and, depending on fortress activity, 50+ visitors. But all is not lost.
   - If the visitor has the artifact you need to hurry. Most of your visitors will hang around for years, but one that took an artifact will leave immediately. Look around near staircases, the exit, and the surroundings. Your military might be able to outrun a weak [goblin](Goblin.md) with a [platinum](Platinum.md) [war hammer](War_hammer.md).
   - If no visitor has a hidden item then the handover hasn't happened yet. You are lucky because the dwarf carrying it will have "no job" for considerable time. Just allocate a huge job order that will take the entire fortress (like dumping all seeds, building 10x10 [floors](Floor.md), creating a massive empty [stone](Stone.md) stockpile without wheelbarrows), and check who does not take up work.
3. Once you have located the contraband you might have a choice how to resolve the issue.
   1. If you want the artifact back you need to let the handover happen. Interrogating the dwarf or the visitor might give a confession but they will keep the item. The visitor will leave with it after serving their sentence, and the dwarf will again try to hand it over.
   2. Assemble a capable military unit at the exit, You do have only one exit, don't you? Keep in mind that visitors might leave through caves.
   3. Once the handover has happened, kill the visitor and re[claim](Claim.md) the item. You may convict the corpse of theft. Depending on your playing style you might want to convict the dwarf as well.

#### Adventure mode

As an adventurer, you may receive quests to retrieve artifacts. The nature of the quest will be as diverse as the nature of artifacts and claimants thereof. Therefore, the difficulty can range from 'piece of cake' to 'nigh-impossible' (if the artifact was claimed, say, by a [demon](Demon.md) lord of a [goblin](Goblin.md) civilization). Once you're in possession of an artifact, you may encounter other parties who have laid a [claim](Claim.md) on it. It is up to you (and the circumstances, e.g. if a third party happens to be well-armed and greater in numbers than yours) to decide which party to satisfy. Alternatively, if the artifact happens to be very useful, such as a strange mood-generated steel sword, you may simply decide to keep it for yourself and leave all troubles behind. Note that completing quests for artifacts will increase your [reputation](Reputation.md), and flaunting your artifacts visibly in front of onlookers will trigger [rumors](Rumor.md).

#### Legends mode

The artifacts' creation history and storage locations are recorded during world generation and can be discovered in [Legends](Legends.md) mode. For example, artifact scepters may become symbols of various rulers.

### Other notes

Artifacts cannot be designated for dumping or melting, nor be damaged by temperature extremes, building destroyers, or fire, although they will ignite and burn as is normal for their material type. They can be lost in pits, carried off the map by flows, stolen by thieves, atom-smashed by bridges, or melted by extreme heat. The loss of an artifact does not appear to affect the happiness of its creator.

In [Dwarf Fortress Talk](../Main/Dwarf_Fortress_Talk.md) [#7](http://www.bay12games.com/media/df_talk_7_transcript.html), Toady stated that even when atom smashed, or melted by extreme heat (or destroyed in any other manner) the artifact will not be deleted but instead receive a "Hidden" flag, which causes the item to respawn in a random location on the site in which it was destroyed, perfectly unharmed, after reclaiming a fort or visiting it in adventure mode. However, in a [Future of the Fortress](../Main/Future_of_the_Fortress.md) post he stated "Artifacts being indestructible by default appears to be gone", due to an "utterly destroyed or transformed" flag.<sup>[\[1\]](http://www.bay12forums.com/smf/index.php?topic=169696.msg8395484#msg8395484)</sup>

After creation, an artifact cannot be further decorated (by encrusting it with gems, studding it with metal, or sewing images into it) - a pitiful gabbro scepter, for instance, cannot be made more valuable.

Artifacts disappear from the artifact list upon abandoning a fortress. Artifact creation has a wider selection of possible item types for several skills, occasionally resulting in artifact items that could not be made normally, like bone shields or metal beds.

Artifacts and their mechanics were the topic of [Dwarf Fortress Talk](../Main/Dwarf_Fortress_Talk.md) [#7](http://www.bay12games.com/media/df_talk_7_transcript.html). The future of artifacts was discussed in a [separate portion](http://www.bay12games.com/media/df_talk_7_transcript_2.html) of that cast.

Artifacts can be disabled in [d_init.txt](D_init.txt.md)

Artifacts created by happy dwarves (i.e. not fell or macabre moods) have a 1% chance to be named after the dwarf who created them.
