**Bismuth bronze** is an alloy of [copper](Copper.md), [tin](Tin.md) and [bismuth](Bismuth.md). It has exactly the same [weapon](Weapon.md) and [armor](Armor.md) properties as regular [bronze](Bronze.md).

### Uses

Just like regular bronze, it can be used to make weapons and armour, along with all [furniture](Furniture.md) and other objects. Bismuth bronze is 20% more [valuable](Value.md) than [bronze](Bronze.md) and three times more [valuable](Value.md) than its component parts.

### Obtaining

Bismuth bronze is made with the following recipe:

- 1 [tin](Tin.md) [bar](Bar.md) + 2 [copper](Copper.md) bars + 1 [bismuth](Bismuth.md) bar (not [ores](Ore.md))

Because of these proportions, bismuth bronze is produced four bars at a time. While the ores must be smelted normally, only one additional [fuel](Fuel.md) is needed to produce those four bars of bismuth bronze from the four bars noted above at a regular [smelter](Smelter.md). At a [magma smelter](Magma_smelter.md), fuel isn't an issue, although the additional time and labor of smelting each ore into bars should still be considered.

Bismuth bronze also provides more weapons-grade metal per tin bar than regular bronze, which may be a consideration if [cassiterite](Cassiterite.md) is rare on your map.

### Trivia

In real life, bismuth bronze is often used in applications where ordinary bronze would corrode. Unlike in Dwarf Fortress, real bismuth bronze typically does not contain more than 1-3% bismuth by weight, though in some cases, up to 6% bismuth may be used.
