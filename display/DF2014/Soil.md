**Soil** is the name for the various kinds of ground that can be [planted](Farming.md) on (both above and below ground) without [irrigation](Irrigation.md) using [water](Water.md). In *Dwarf Fortress*, the category of "Soil" includes all types of sand, [clay](Clay.md), ooze and any "non-stone" layer equally, even if you or I generally don't associate that substance with "growing plants".

**Notes:**

- The "topsoil" types listed in the upper part of the table do **not** count as [sand](Sand.md) for [glassmaking](Glass.md), even if their names include the word "sand" - only those in the "Sand" section are usable for this purpose.
- The 3 "ocean floor" layers are only found under deep [ocean](Ocean.md) tiles, usually far out from any embarkable site. Thus, they can rarely, if ever be encountered, in either [fortress mode](Fortress_mode.md) or [adventurer mode](Adventurer_mode.md).
- Soil layers marked with ≈ are capable of supporting an [aquifer](Aquifer.md).
- The [clay](Clay.md) types listed at the bottom can be used for making ceramic items. Fire clay produces [stoneware](Stoneware.md), while the other types produce [earthenware](Earthenware.md).

In previous versions, types and quantities of available soils were listed at the bottom right of the fortress location selection screen; in the current version, however, it will only indicate the presence of soil and whether it can be used as [clay](Clay.md).

Digging into soil does not generate any byproduct materials, unlike digging in [rock](Stone.md), and also is a much faster process which makes it much easier to create storerooms and other large areas of empty space, and to train [miners](Miner.md).

Soil cannot be [smoothed](Smoothing.md), which makes it more difficult to make high-value rooms or pierce [aquifers](Aquifer.md), and impossible to carve [fortifications](Fortification.md), [tracks](Track.md), or [engravings](Engraving.md).

Building a [farm plot](Farm_plot.md) or [road](Road.md) or removing a [construction](Construction.md) on top of soil will cause it to become "furrowed", making it appear with the `≈` and `~` tiles. Furrowed soil — including furrowed subterranean soil — will gradually smooth itself out, at which point grass and other vegetation will begin to grow. Sand always appears as `≈` and `~` and cannot become furrowed - as such, sand roads do not last as long as those made on ordinary soil.

#### Trees and Shrubs

Once you discover any [cavern](Cavern.md) layer, all subterranean [grass](Grass.md), [shrubs](Shrub.md), and [trees](Tree.md) growing within that cavern will begin to grow on any subterranean soil.

### Kaleidoscopic soil

If the game somehow fails to assign any soil materials to the current region's geological layer definitions (e.g. if the `[SOIL]` or `[SOIL_SAND]` tokens are removed), then when the game attempts to determine the material used by a given soil tile, it will return a **random number** in order to alert the user that something has gone wrong. As a result, the tile's appearance will rapidly change, and [mining](Mining.md) will yield a random [stone](Stone.md), [gem](Gem.md), or unusable boulder made of [metal](Metal.md) or other [generated material](Material.md) and leave behind a floor composed of the original layer material, assuming the mining job does not randomly cancel due to the selection of an undiggable material such as [slade](Slade.md).

The same thing can happen with layer stone tiles if no materials are marked with an appropriate layer type (such as `[IGNEOUS_EXTRUSIVE]` or `[SEDIMENTARY]`).
