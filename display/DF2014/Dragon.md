> *A gigantic reptilian creature. It is magical and can breathe fire. These monsters can live for thousands of years.*

**Dragons** are massive, [fire](Fire.md)-breathing [megabeasts](Megabeast.md) who, at their full size, are the [second largest](List_of_creatures_by_adult_size.md#end-of-list) land [creatures](Creature.md) in the world (behind the [giant elephant](Giant_elephant.md)). They are covetous monsters and seek to [steal items](Steals_items.md) from your fortresses to bring back to their lairs. Dragons generally pilfer successfully in [worldgen](World_generation.md), but when they attack a player fortress, they will simply wipe the place out or die trying.

Dragons are most notable for their ability to quickly and frequently exhale long (20+ tiles) jets of [dragonfire](Dragonfire.md), a much-hotter (theoretically 50000 °U, over four times the heat of [magma](Magma.md)) version of fire, which can make quick work of any creature caught in its path and can kill even creatures normally immune to fire, though it can be blocked by enemies equipped with [shields](Shield.md). All dragons are born with Talented [skill](Skill.md) in [fighting, biting, kicking, dodging](Combat_skill.md) and [observing](Observer.md), which, combined with their size and dragonfire, makes them formidable opponents to an untrained [military](Military.md) [squad](Squad.md).

While based on the western dragon model, dragons neither fly nor have wings, and are not intelligent (though there is room to address this; see [Bugs and Future Plans](#irregularities2c-bugs2c-and-future-plans)). They are often described as [glass cannons](http://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/GlassCannon), as they are not particularly durable and can be defeated somewhat easily with skilled soldiers or [traps](Trap.md), but only in comparison to their fellow megabeasts; a single dragon can easily wipe out an unprepared fortress. Dragons are associated with the [spheres](Sphere.md) of fire and wealth, and their names will often reflect that.

They are not to be confused with the [cave dragons](Cave_dragon.md) - a separate, non-megabeast species.

Some dwarves [like](Preferences.md) dragons for their *terrible majesty*.

### Dragon Size

Dragons slowly grow to become one of the largest creatures in the game, finally reaching their adult size of 25,000,000 cm<sup>3</sup> after 1000 years. As a hatchling, dragons are quite tiny, at exactly 1/10th an adult dwarf's size, but they grow very rapidly, at roughly 25,000 cm<sup>3</sup> per year. Dragons reach dwarven size shortly after their second birthday, are more than double a dwarf's size at about year 5, and add another dwarf's-worth in size roughly every two years after that.

- At birth, a dragon is the size of a [fox](Fox.md).
- At 2 years, a dragon is the size of a [dwarf](Dwarf.md).
- At 23 years, a dragon is the size of a [giant cheetah](Giant_cheetah.md).
- At 100 years, a dragon is the size of a [draltha](Draltha.md).
- At 200 years, a dragon is the size of an [elephant](Elephant.md).
- At 320 years, a dragon is the size of a [hydra](Hydra.md).
- At 800 years, a dragon is the size of a [bronze colossus](Bronze_colossus.md) or an adult [roc](Roc.md).
- Thereafter, a dragon is the second largest creature found on land. (The [giant elephant](Giant_elephant.md) is the largest land creature, while the [giant sperm whale](Giant_sperm_whale.md) is the largest creature found anywhere.)

### Defense strategies

Dragonfire can be blocked if the victim is using a shield, and it will be more than 99% of the time. Though they are among the physically weakest of the megabeasts, dragons are still massively powerful in melee combat, so they can be hard to take down without a good military. They should never be engaged in areas covered in [grass](Grass.md) or [cave moss](Cave_moss.md), as their breath attacks will cause fire to spread across **all** grass in the area, consuming your map in flames, causing [trees](Tree.md) to fall, and, above all else, murdering your [FPS](FPS.md). When attacked by a dragon, lure it underground, preferably into an area made out of stone floors away from any [farm plots](Farm_plot.md). While they are immune to fire damage, dragons can still [drown](Swimmer.md) if immersed in magma.

Dragonfire can be avoided by fighting the dragon in a hallway that is covered in 4/7 water depth, here the dragon can not spit fire leaving it open for melee combat.

Possibly the best defense is to use piercing weapons like crossbows, and especially spears, and hope you get lucky and hit a vital organ which can bring it down immediately. An alternative is building one or multiple cage traps, possibly beforehand, which will probably cage the dragon making it harmless. You should know that cages and mechanisms will be destroyed by dragonfire unless constructed from pure slade, so either make sure both cages (or weapons, or supports) and mechanisms are impervious to dragonfire, or, more simply, ensure that the dragon has no reason to breathe fire by removing animals and dwarves from the trapped area. While they won't fire on buildings normally, a stray blast (at your outdoor livestock or bait animals, say) that catches any traps, doors, or bridges not made of dragonfire-safe materials will melt and deactivate them. This can be used to your advantage, since dragons will destroy the bridge they are standing on, even if they aren't aiming for it. Also, dragons make good companions if you are able to reanimate them as a [necromancer](Necromancer.md).

### Domestication

[![](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/thumb/c/cc/Dragonprev.png/260px-Dragonprev.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Dragonprev.png)

[https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Dragonprev.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Dragonprev.png)

How majestic!<br>*Art by Olivitree*

Dragons can be captured in [cage](Cage.md) [traps](Trap.md) and [trained](Animal_trainer.md) if you're lucky enough to catch one. Currently, knowledge of animal behaviour is based on the civilization level and depends partly on the animals available for contact in a given civilization site. Such a rare megabeast as a dragon is unlikely to have had any civilized contact aside from adventurers attempting to slay them and other violent contact; as such, your fort will have to build the knowledge base from the ground up, making dragon-training a highly difficult task. Bear in mind that even with a skilled animal trainer at hand, your first attempts to control such a powerful and elusive beast may result in [half your fortress burning in dragonfire](Fun.md). Being megabeasts, dragons are perpetually hostile to your military [squads](Squad.md) and must be kept separated from your soldiers, otherwise they will kill each other on sight.<sup>[Bug](#bugs):[10731](https://dwarffortressbugtracker.com/view.php?id=10731)</sup>

If you can manage to endure its long-untamed wrath, you'll have a massively valuable pet that can lay [eggs](Egg.md). Dragons can also be trained as war or hunting animals at an [animal training zone](Activity_zone.md#animal-training). While a trained dragon is an immensely destructive tool at your disposal, it is also very eager to use dragonfire against your enemies, which can be *exceedingly* [fun](Fun.md) if it happens in your [grass](Pasture.md), [booze](Alcohol.md) [stockpile](Stockpile.md) or [meeting area](Meeting_area.md).

Provided you are lucky enough to capture a pair of dragons of different sexes, you can [breed](Egg.md#hatching-conditions) them with a [nest box](Nest_box.md). Hatched dragons will immediately be considered adults, though they will still need to progress through 1,000 years of growth to reach full size. As they are born adults, it isn't possible to create [fully tame](Animal_trainer.md#taming-children) dragons without [modding](Modding.md).

### Irregularities, Bugs, and Future Plans

[![](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/7/70/Dragon_Size.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Dragon_Size.png)

Estimated size comparison between a fully-grown dragon and a dwarf.

Dragons have been observed to occasionally wear some [armor](Armor.md) (breastplates, greaves, leggings and boots). This armor is specified as "Large \[metal\]\[armor type\]" and gives the dragon the same protection as any other species might get from it. This is a bug, as dragons can't normally equip items.

[Toady One](../Main/Toady_One.md) has mentioned that he plans to eventually extend the random creature generator of the game to create different species and varieties of dragon within certain constraints, calling it "Half-Random", with ideas for variants including just about anything dragons have been given in literature, such as acidic blood, while maintaining a basic draconic structure.<sup>([\[1\]](http://www.bay12forums.com/smf/index.php?topic=30026.msg1012311#msg1012311))</sup> He has also commented on the game's current portrayal of dragons as wingless unintelligent creatures, citing his reasons to be the many ways dragons are depicted in real-life sources.(<sup>[\[2\]](http://www.bay12forums.com/smf//index.php?topic=159164.msg7156980#msg7156980)</sup>)

One of the [old power goals](../Main/Consolidated_development~2.md#power-goals) referenced stealing dragon eggs as part of an [adventurer mode](Adventurer_mode.md) quest.

Contrary to popular belief, a dragon is not in fact toothless, and will tear you to shreds, resulting in [fun](Fun.md).

They will, in accordance to popular belief, however, set fire to the countryside, [peasants](Peasant.md), and any thatched roof cottages that happen to be in the vicinity.

Though dragons are thought to make their lairs in dungeons, actual sightings of "Dragons in Dungeons" are extremely rare.

Also contrary to popular belief, dragonfire is in fact not actually fire, but is composed of boiling, molten clouds of varied metals such as: silver, copper, steel, etc... So one could argue that dragons feed on minerals and armored adventurers to produce their breath, or that dragons can breath plasma.

Dragonfire is also known to make trade depots explode.
