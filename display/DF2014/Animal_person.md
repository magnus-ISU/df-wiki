**Animal people** are half-man, half-beast [creatures](Creature.md) with various animal-like traits, some beneficial (such as [flying](Flying.md), and [poisons](Syndrome.md)), or detrimental ([thievery](Steals_items.md)), inherited from their base animal. They generally have significantly longer [lifespans](Age.md) than the base animals, as well as the ability to [learn](Learns.md). They can be legless, but get arms even if their base animal is limbless. Their body size is the average of their base animal's size and 70,000 (the size of a [human](Human.md)), working out so that the smallest animal people are 35,000cm³, half the size of a [human](Human.md), but they can get [very large](Sperm_whale_man.md). Animal people can join [civilizations](Civilization.md) and live in [sites](Site.md), or come to your fort as [visitors](Visitor.md).

Animal people are playable creatures in [adventure mode](Adventure_mode.md). If they became established members of a playable civ (dwarf, elf, human), they will appear listed in the creature selection screen alongside the main races. Otherwise, they are selectable under Intelligent Wilderness Creature. Some animal people may not be available due to having [died out](Extinction.md) or being uncontacted (e.g. excluded to an isolated island).

Wild animal people wander on the surface in [savage](Savage.md) environments or dwell underground in the [caverns](Cavern.md); these two populations form distinct groups. Subterranean animal people are actually a civilization in themselves, rarely encountered, in small groups or "tribes". They are equipped with primitive weaponry such as wooden [spears](Spear.md) and [blowguns](Blowgun.md) (they are the only known source of [blowdarts](Blowdart.md)). Subterranean animal people will be labelled as Hostile upon discovery – they are not interested in negotiations, and will attack your dwarves on sight. Encountering them often comes as a surprise, since there is no way to know about their presence before embarking. They do not appear in the civilizations [menu](Menu.md).

Subterranean animal people are also found in generated [sewers](Sewer.md), formed from collections of outcasts.

#### Ethics

Being an entity in their own right, subterranean animal people possess ethics; much like other civs. Those particular ethics are incomplete for now — being directly copied from [kobolds](Kobold.md) and their [values](Personality_trait.md) all set to zero (none).

Like kobolds, subterranean animal people consider the killing of other living beings acceptable, but treason is unthinkable, and they'll respond to the murder of their own kind by exiling the offender from their tribe. They oppose torturing for information and the torture of animals, but consider torturing for fun appropriate. Assault is considered a personal matter, while slavery, the taking of animal trophies, and the consumption of sapient beings (which includes other animal people) are all unthinkable acts.

### History

Present from the first official release of the game, the animal people did not exist as a class but were instead part of a small group of subterranean creatures that harassed your dwarves. [Frogmen](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/23a:Frogman), [lizardmen](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/23a:Lizardman), and [snakemen](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/23a:Snakeman) emerged from [cave rivers](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/23a:Cave_river), and [antmen](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/23a:Antman), [batmen](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/23a:Batman), and [ratmen](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/23a:Ratman) would come out from [chasms](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/23a:Chasm) to attack.

[v0.27.169.32a](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/40d:Release_information) released three-dimensional worlds, and subterranean creatures were moved to flock near generated [chasms](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/40d:Chasm), [underground rivers](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/40d:Underground_river), and [underground pools](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/40d:Underground_pool). [Cave swallowmen](Cave_swallow_man.md) and [olmmen](Olm_man.md) were introduced. The first set of above-ground savage animal people were also introduced: [leechmen](Leech_man.md), [snailmen](Snail_man.md), [slugmen](Slug_man.md), and [tigermen](Tiger_man.md).

In [v0.31](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/v0.31:Release_information), every creature was renamed and/or had their properties revamped (for example, "frogman" was changed to "amphibian man" and "batman" was changed to "bat man"). Not all animal people were redefined in this version, and some continued to use old codes for many releases. The version also introduced "[creature variation](Creature_variation_token.md)" templates, which made converting existing animals to humanoid (and giant) variations much more efficient. Underground features were expanded to the current [caverns](Cavern.md), and subterranean animal people formed civilizations. More anthropomorphic variations of animals present at the time were added.

More animal people were added to the game in [v0.34](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/v0.34:Release_information).

[v0.42.01](Release_information/0.42.01.md) allowed animal people to join civilizations, live in sites, and visit forts. Animal people became playable as adventurers. (A grizzly bear hammerman has much to offer a dwarven society.) [v0.42.04](Release_information/0.42.04.md) added more animal people to the game.
