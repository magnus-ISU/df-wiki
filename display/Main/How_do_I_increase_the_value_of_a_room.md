[Room value](Zone.md#quality-and-value) can be essential, whether it is a high-class [dining hall](Dining_hall.md) for your dwarves or opulent throne rooms for your [nobles](Noble.md). Depending on what resources you have available, there are several ways to quickly [increase the value](Zone.md#building-optimally) of a room:

- Increase the size of the room - doubling the amount of floorspace nearly doubles the base value of the room (before furniture is added in).

- [Smoothing](Smoothing.md) and [engraving](Engraving.md) the walls and floors. Note: when engraving walls, make sure your engravers are working from \*inside\* the room. Also note that smoothing enhances the value of walls more than that of floors, so you may want an oddly-shaped room with a lot of walls. Smoothing also increases the value of clusters/veins more than that of layer stone, even if the cluster stone has the same material value as the layer stone -- see [Kipi's research](http://dffd.wimbli.com/file.php?id=7326).

- Adding [artifact](Artifact.md) furniture - a single legendary piece of furniture can greatly increase the value of a room.

- Adding high-value furniture - [masterwork](Quality.md#masterwork) furniture is worth more than twice as much as the next highest level. [Statues](Statue.md) have the highest [base value](Item_value.md) of "normal" furniture, while [weapon traps](Weapon_trap.md) allow you to stack 11 high-value items into one convenient tile, and [levers](Lever.md) allow stacking theoretically unlimited numbers of [mechanisms](Mechanism.md).

- Adding more furniture - even low-quality furniture - can raise the room value, if you use enough of it. Fill the room with whatever leftovers are available and watch the value climb.

- Building [floor bars](Floor_bars.md) only requires the [furniture hauling](Furniture_hauling.md) skill, and can produce a modest increase in room value, particularly when expensive metal bars are used. Your metal bars can be reclaimed later if needed.

- Make sure the room is fully enclosed by walls and/or doors -- gaps in the perimeter reduce the value of certain features of the room by a percentage -- see [here](http://www.bay12forums.com/smf/index.php?topic=124938) for details.
