<br>

The `[OBJECT:CREATURE]` [token](Token.md) begins the definition of a *Dwarf Fortress* [creature](Creature.md) [raw file](Raw_file.md). Each creature definition that follows begins with the `[CREATURE:creature ID]` token, and the creature's exact properties and behavior are then specified by the use of further creature tokens. All known creature tokens are listed below.

Vanilla creature definitions can be found in `<Dwarf Fortress>\data\vanilla\vanilla_creatures\`. Creature ID is also used with [graphics tokens](Graphics_token.md) to make customizable [graphics sets](Graphics_set.md).

The [caste](Caste.md) tokens allow defining sub-species within the broader creature definition, including true biological castes and lesser variations, such as sexes. Creature tokens can either be 'Creature' or 'CASTE-only' type which can only be applied to creature or caste respectively, or 'CASTE' which can be applied to both. In the table bellow, creature type distinguishes tokens that can be applied to creature only, caste only, and both ('creature', 'CASTE-only', and 'CASTE' respectively)

[BODY:BODY_WITH_HEAD_FLAG:HEART:GUTS:BRAIN:MOUTH] <br/>

<i>HEIGHT</i> : marks the height to be changed <br/>

</p>

A <a href="Purring_maggot.md">maggot</a> would only need:<br/>

<tr>
<td> <a href="#frequency">FREQUENCY</a>
</td>
<td>Creature
</td>
<td>
<ul><li>number, max 100</li></ul>
</td>
<td>The <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#frequency">[FREQUENCY]</a></code> value plays two separate roles. The first is in determining the initial distribution of creatures across the world map. Each creature is randomly assigned a single x, y co-ordinate on the world map, which act as the epicenter for that creature's distribution. A square is drawn around that x, y co-ordinate with a Manhattan radius equal to the <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#frequency">[FREQUENCY]</a></code> value divided by 100 times the world map size. For example, in a 256 by 256 size world map, the <a href="Lion.md">lion</a> might be assigned 14, 112. The <a href="Lion.md">lion</a> has <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#frequency5">[FREQUENCY:5]</a></code>, and so a square is drawn by moving 13 tiles in each direction from the <a href="Lion.md">lion</a>'s x, y co-ordinate. This is the <a href="Lion.md">lion</a>'s "territory".
<p>Each sub-region in the world will attempt to fill lists of wildlife. There are five lists - <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#vermin-grounder">[VERMIN_GROUNDER]</a></code>, <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#vermin-soil">[VERMIN_SOIL]</a></code>, <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#vermin-soil-colony">[VERMIN_SOIL_COLONY]</a></code>, <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#large-roaming">[LARGE_ROAMING]</a></code>, and <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#large-predator">[LARGE_PREDATOR]</a></code>, corresponding with the relevant tokens. The game will attempt to place seven creatures in each list for each sub-region. The game will select the seven nearest valid creatures. Creatures are valid for sub-region's list if they have the requisite token for that list, if they have the valid token for that sub-region's biome (for example, the [lion] can only be selected for <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#biomesavanna-tropical">[BIOME:SAVANNA_TROPICAL]</a></code>, <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#biomegrassland-tropical">[BIOME:GRASSLAND_TROPICAL]</a></code>, and <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#biomeshrubland-tropical">[BIOME:SHRUBLAND_TROPICAL]</a></code>, and if their "territory" as defined by their random epicenter and <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#frequency">[FREQUENCY]</a></code> radius overlaps with that sub-region. These lists then determine the creatures that can actually appear within that sub-region during gameplay.
</p><p>There are some exceptions to the above. If the game was not capable of filling all seven entries in a list, it will drop the overlapping territory requirement, and simply pull the nearest creature which has the correct token and biome availability. Conversely, if a creature has an epicenter but has not appeared on any of the list for any of the world map's sub-regions, the creature will be assigned to the relevant list for the nearest appropriate sub-region - meaning it is occasionally possible to have lists of eight creatures or more. This is more common in smaller worlds where there are less possible sub-regions to be assigned towards. Creatures with the <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#good">[GOOD]</a></code> and <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#evil">[EVIL]</a></code> tokens ignore the epicenter distribution system altogether. They are always capable of appearing in appropriate biomes which are <code><a href="Building_token.md#good">[GOOD]</a></code> or <code><a href="Building_token.md#evil">[EVIL]</a></code> respectively. This is not true for <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#savage">[SAVAGE]</a></code>, which acts more like the biome tokens. Creatures with <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#ubiquitous">[UBIQUITOUS]</a></code> have a "territory" which covers the entire map, regardless of their epicenter (although they can still fail to be chosen if there are 7 creatures which are eligible and have nearer epicenters to the sub-region in question).
</p><p>The second use for <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#frequency">[FREQUENCY]</a></code> is to determine how often a creature actually appears on map. In Fortress Mode, the game will try and spawn large wildlife (creatures with <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#large-roaming">[LARGE_ROAMING]</a></code> or <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#large-predator">[LARGE_PREDATOR]</a></code> in fairly regular waves. These waves include <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#large-roaming">[LARGE_ROAMING]</a></code>, <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#large-roaming">[LARGE_ROAMING]</a></code> combined with <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#flier">[FLIER]</a></code>, <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#large-predator">[LARGE_PREDATOR]</a></code>, and <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#curious-beast">[CURIOUS_BEAST]</a></code> - so a <a href="Lion.md">lion</a> does not compete for selection with a <a href="Gazelle.md">gazelle</a>. When the game decides it needs to spawn in a fresh wave of e.g. <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#large-roaming">[LARGE_ROAMING]</a></code> creatures, it will select one of the creatures available to it from the lists for that sub-region at random, with all creatures weighted equally. Once it has selected a creature, it then effectively rolls a d100 against the relevant creature's <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#frequency">[FREQUENCY]</a></code>. If the d100 is equal to the creature's <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#frequency">[FREQUENCY]</a></code> or less, that creature is then spawned in. If the d100 is above the creature's <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#frequency">[FREQUENCY]</a></code>, the game returns to the relevant list and selects again. <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#ubiquitous">[UBIQUITOUS]</a></code> acts as <code><a href="Creature_token~2.md#frequency100">[FREQUENCY:100]</a></code> for these purposes - in other words, the creature cannot fail the d100 check and will always be spawned in if it is selected from the list.
</p><p>Defaults to 50.
</p>
</td></tr></tbody></table>

<td>Defines a gait by which the creature can move. See <a href="Gait.md">Gait</a> for more information. Specifically, you likely want to use one of the existing STANDARD_X_GAITS creature variations, as described in <a href="Gait.md#gaits-and-modding">this subsection</a>.
<ul><li>&lt;max speed&gt; indicates the maximum <a href="Gait.md#speed">speed</a> achievable by a creature using this gait</li>
<li>&lt;start speed&gt; indicates the creature's speed when it starts moving using this gait</li>
<li>&lt;build up time&gt; indicates how long it will take for a creature using this gait to go from &lt;start speed&gt; to &lt;max speed&gt;. For example, a value of 10 means that it should be able to reach the maximum speed by moving 10 <a href="Tile.md">tiles</a> in a straight line over even terrain.</li>
<li>&lt;max turning speed&gt; indicates the maximum speed permissible when the creature suddenly changes its direction of motion. The creature's speed will be reduced to &lt;max turning speed&gt; if travelling at a higher speed than this before turning.</li>
<li>&lt;energy expenditure&gt; indicates how energy-consuming the gait is. Higher values cause the creature to tire out faster. Persistent usage of a high-intensity gait will eventually lead to exhaustion and <a href="Status_icon.md#non-flashing">collapse</a>.</li></ul>
<p><b>NO_BUILD_UP</b> can be specified instead of a &lt;start speed&gt; value to make the &lt;max speed&gt; instantly achievable upon initiating movement (this is equivalent to a &lt;build up time&gt; of 0). Note that &lt;build up time&gt; and &lt;max turning speed&gt; are both ignored if specified alongside this (as NO_BUILD_UP trumps &lt;build up time&gt; and preserves &lt;max speed&gt; whilst turning, and &lt;max turning speed&gt; cannot exceed &lt;max speed&gt;) so it is permissible to omit them so long as they are <b>both</b> omitted together.
</p><p>It's possible to specify a &lt;start speed&gt; greater than the &lt;max speed&gt;; the moving creature will decelerate towards its &lt;max speed&gt; in this case.
</p><p><br/>
<b>valid gait types:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>WALK</b><br/></li></ul>
<p>Used for moving normally over ground tiles.
</p>
<ul><li><b>CRAWL</b><br/></li></ul>
<p>Used for moving over ground tiles whilst <a href="Status_icon.md#non-flashing">prone</a>.
</p>
<ul><li><b>SWIM</b><br/></li></ul>
<p>Used for moving through tiles containing <a href="Water.md">water</a> or <a href="Magma.md">magma</a> at a <a href="Water.md#depth">depth</a> of at least 4/7. 
</p>
<ul><li><b>FLY</b><br/></li></ul>
<p>Used for moving through <a href="Open_space.md">open space</a>.
</p>
<ul><li><b>CLIMB</b><br/></li></ul>
<p>Used for moving whilst <a href="Climber.md">climbing</a>.
</p><p><br/>
<b>valid gait flags:</b>
</p>
<ul><li><b>AGILITY</b><br/></li></ul>
<p>Speeds/slows movement depending on the creature's <a href="Attribute.md#agility">Agility</a> stat.
</p>
<ul><li><b>STRENGTH</b><br/></li></ul>
<p>Speeds/slows movement depending on the creature's <a href="Attribute.md#strength">Strength</a> stat.
</p>
<ul><li><b>LAYERS_SLOW</b><br/></li></ul>
<p>Makes <a href="Tissue_definition_token.md#thickens-on-energy-storage">THICKENS_ON_ENERGY_STORAGE</a> and <a href="Tissue_definition_token.md#thickens-on-strength">THICKENS_ON_STRENGTH</a> tissue layers slow movement depending on how thick they are. </p>
<ul><li><b>STEALTH_SLOWS:</b>&lt;percentage&gt;<br/></li></ul>
<p>Slows movement by the specified percentage when the creature is <a href="Ambusher.md">sneaking</a>.
</p>

### Tissue Layer Tokens

Tissue layers are added to a creature's body parts by the creature tokens [TISSUE_LAYER](#tissue-layer), [TISSUE_LAYER_OVER](#tissue-layer-over), [TISSUE_LAYER_UNDER](#tissue-layer-under), and the body detail plan token [TL_LAYERS](#tl-layers).

These tissue layers are not the same thing as tissues, which are defined at creature level, but rather applications of the tissues. If the SKIN tissue of a creature is the more general and abstract notion of what skin is for that creature, then a tissue layer may be the "actual" skin on the creature's nose, head, or second toe. As most creatures have more than one body part, tissue layers are normally selected en masse.

Some tissue layer tokens are analogous to tissue definition tokens, e.g. [TL_CONNECTS](#tl-connects) to [CONNECTS](Tissue_definition_token.md#connects).
