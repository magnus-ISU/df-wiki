Enemies in [sieges](Siege.md) will often arrive riding on other [creatures](Creature.md), their **mounts**. These mounts can not only add to the attacks of those mounted on them, but may also give their riders more abilities: their mounts might be able to [fly](Flying.md) or [swim](Swimmer.md), be [building destroyers](Building_destroyer.md), prevent the operation of [bridges](Bridge.md), be faster and fleeter of foot, or just be plain scary in combat.

Depending on the type of creature that can carry out a siege, different races will have different mounts that are somewhat unique to them.

### Types

[![](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/thumb/e/e5/Mounted_combat.jpeg/244px-Mounted_combat.jpeg)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Mounted_combat.jpeg)

[https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Mounted_combat.jpeg](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Mounted_combat.jpeg)

"...and the severed part flies off in an arc!"

Enemy [squads](Squad.md) can appear with a single mount for the squad [captain](Captain.md), or individual mounts for each member of the squad (potentially including more than one species of mount). Some mounts such as [giant bats](Giant_bat~2.md) or [giant cave swallows](Giant_cave_swallow.md) possess the ability to fly, pathing over whatever walls and traps you have set up for them. This can lead to a great deal of [fun](Fun.md) if you do not have sufficient marksdwarves to deal with them. Some siegers also come on amphibious mounts, such as [cave crocodiles](Cave_crocodile.md) and [giant toads](Giant_toad.md). Humorously, these units will disregard their rider's inability to breathe underwater or [swim](Swimmer.md), and reports of amphibious mounts pathing into [moats](Moat.md) or [rivers](River.md), only to have their goblin riders drown, are fairly common.<sup>[Bug](#bugs):[926](https://dwarffortressbugtracker.com/view.php?id=926)</sup>

There are three types of mounts: ordinary mounts ridden by [humans](Human.md); above-ground (or "good") exotic mounts ridden by [elves](Elf.md); and underground (or "evil") exotic mounts ridden by [goblins](Goblin.md). There are many creatures that have `[MOUNT]` or `[MOUNT_EXOTIC]` in their raw files, but below are some of the most common when an army besieges your fortress.

### Who rides what

- [Goblin](Goblin.md) sieges have been known to ride [beak dogs](Beak_dog.md), [elk birds](Elk_bird.md), [giant cave swallows](Giant_cave_swallow.md), [giant olms](Giant_olm.md), [giant bats](Giant_bat~2.md), [cave crocodiles](Cave_crocodile.md), [rutherers](Rutherer.md), [jabberers](Jabberer.md), [blind cave bears](Blind_cave_bear.md), and [voracious cave crawlers](Voracious_cave_crawler.md). These are usually the only sieges where you need to worry about enemies flying into your fortress from above.

- [Elves](Elf.md) prefer arriving on foot, but when they do ride mounts, they usually use the dreaded [unicorn](Unicorn.md), as well as various big cats. It is theoretically possible for them to arrive riding any exotic land animal, such as [elephants](Elephant.md), [tigers](Tiger.md), [unicorns](Unicorn.md), or even [giant mantises](Giant_mantis.md). Because of this, be prepared for an occasional flying mount when elves besiege your fortress.

- [Human](Human.md) sieges will come riding [camels](Camel.md) (of both varieties), [horses](Horse.md), and [grizzly bears](Grizzly_bear.md), in addition to bringing along squads of trained war animals, like [giant cheetahs](Giant_cheetah.md).

- [Dwarves](Dwarf.md) may field cavalry similar to human and goblin forces, should they have the luck to domesticate something.

Beyond acting like a force multiplier, dead mounts can be [butchered](Butcher.md) after the battle, to feed your [meat industry](Meat_industry.md). A squad of cave dragons that is killed and hauled back inside your fort will produce a *lot* of meat products, making mounted sieges convenient food resupplies - if you are prepared to handle them, that is. Note that goblins only have a single animal trainer, their general; if he falls (or, because of a bug, leaves the map), you will never see mounted goblin units again.

Note that in the vanilla game, playable civs *cannot*, under any circumstances, field cavalry in fortress mode; this applies to all races, not just dwarves.

It is possible for adventurers and their companions to make use of mounts, which may be taken in character generation or acquired over the course of the game. To climb onto (or off of) a mount press [![Adv ui h.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/4/42/Adv_ui_h.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Adv_ui_h.png)h while standing next to it. You can also [![Adv ui j.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/5/5a/Adv_ui_j.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Adv_ui_j.png)j jump off a mount or dismount with the dodge option in the [![Adv ui A.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/9/95/Adv_ui_A.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Adv_ui_A.png)A Aim attack menu. (Unfortunately, the speed the mount is travelling does not increase the distance you can jump, unlike when you are sprinting on foot.)

You can also lead a mount as an option in the [![Adv ui h.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/4/42/Adv_ui_h.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Adv_ui_h.png)h hold menu. This may keep them from running off, but will reduce your speed (even if the animal can move faster).

You can [![Adv ui p.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/f/f9/Adv_ui_p.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Adv_ui_p.png)p pack items onto a mount, or [![Adv ui g.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/f/f7/Adv_ui_g.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Adv_ui_g.png)g get an item packed onto it, when mounted or next to them. This can be useful for carrying provisions for long journeys, or returning with heavy trophies, treasures, or goods.

Mounts are listed on the [![Adv ui c.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/f/f9/Adv_ui_c.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Adv_ui_c.png)c сompanions list with their location relative to you. See [Adventurer mode gameplay#Companions](Adventurer_mode_gameplay.md#companions) for details.

With [![Adv ui X.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/e/e5/Adv_ui_X.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Adv_ui_X.png)X-a you can pet your mounts (and indeed any animal, including undead ones, although the latter is generally not advised). This may give your character a happy [thought](Thought.md), but does not appear to have any effect on the animal. Note that not all creatures you can play as adventurers have the Pet animal ability.

A mount can be quite advantageous, as animals that are appropriate as mounts are generally very large, and therefore quite dangerous in combat. They also allow you to travel faster, and may allow you to outrun enemies that would otherwise be impossible to catch (or escape from.) You can change your mount's speed in the [![Adv ui m.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/8/84/Adv_ui_m.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Adv_ui_m.png)m speed and [![Adv ui S.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/1/1b/Adv_ui_S.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Adv_ui_S.png)S stealth menu while mounted. Mounted creatures still have minds of their own, however, and may panic and run away (or decide to attack) at inopportune times. The [Rider](Rider.md) skill seems to affect how likely this is to happen, but further verification is required.

You can adopt any stray mount found loitering around civilized sites, simply by mounting it. You can also Claim it as a [pet](Pet.md), which will cause it to show up on your map even if you aren't able to mount it initially.

The only known way to mount a non-tamed non-stray creature is to first become a [necromancer](Necromancer.md), then kill the creature, and raise it as your [undead slave](Undead.md). Both [dragons](Dragon.md) and [rocs](Roc.md) can be ridden this way. Only necromancer adventurers may ride undead mounts.

Interestingly, pets aren't part of a civilization and have no civ ID, the only [historical figure](Historical_figure.md) link they get is that the adventurer is their owner. On the other hand, the adventurer doesn't have a corresponding link to the pet, so if a member of your party asks another member about their pet, they will answer that they don't know anything about them.

The [Ambusher](Ambusher.md) skill doesn't level up while sneaking on a mount; instead it trains up the mount's skills (assuming they [CAN_LEARN](Learning.md)).

Mounts can [give birth](Breeding.md) in Adventurer Mode, and may do so in the midst of battle.

In Adventurer Mode, dwarves and humans can ride [horses](Horse.md) and both varieties of camel. However, they cannot ride [donkeys](Donkey.md) or [mules](Mule.md) (despite their similarity to horses and real-life use as mounts; probably since donkeys and mules weren't commonly used as *war* mounts) or [alpacas](Alpaca.md), [water buffalo](Water_buffalo.md), or [reindeer](Reindeer.md).
