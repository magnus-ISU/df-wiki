> *For "blocking" in combat, see [Armor](Armor.md).*

A **block** is a type of building material that is more architecturally efficient than its non-processed, raw material counterpart. Blocks are lighter, more valuable, and build faster. This makes blocks especially useful for large construction projects. A [ceramic](Ceramic.md) (fired from [clay](Clay.md)) block is called a **brick** but behaves the same as all other blocks.

[Stone](Stone.md), [wood](Wood.md), [glass](Glass.md), [ceramic](Ceramic.md), and [metal](Metal.md) blocks can be created respectively in a [Stoneworker's workshop](Stoneworker's_workshop.md), a [carpenter's workshop](Carpenter's_workshop~2.md), a [glass furnace](Glass_furnace.md), a [kiln](Kiln.md), and a [forge](Forge.md). One stone or a wooden log can be shaped into 4 blocks, which makes them more useful for larger constructions. All other materials produce only 1 block/task. All blocks share the same advantages in construction, relative value and weight.

Blocks can be used as building materials for [roads](Road.md), [bridges](Bridge.md), [workshops](Workshop.md) and all types of [constructions](Construction.md). When a raw material (e.g. raw stone rock) is used instead, the game will describe the result as "rough", while buildings built from blocks are not given that adjective.

- Note that blocks are **required** to build [wells](Well.md), [screw pumps](Screw_pump.md), and [asheries](Ashery.md).

Even though blocks are quite useful in terms of construction, they cannot replace other functions of the raw material. For example, stone blocks cannot be used for making furniture or craft goods, tools such as [pots](Pot.md) or [jugs](Jug.md), [mechanisms](Mechanism.md), or [catapult](Catapult.md) ammunition. Nor can wooden blocks be used to make furniture, craft goods, tools, siege weapon components, ballista arrowheads, or crossbow bolts, or be burned at a [wood furnace](Wood_furnace~2.md). Metal blocks can't be used for [metalsmith's forge](Metalsmith's_forge.md) jobs (i.e. making armor or weapons, etc) but **can** be [melted](Melt.md) down at a [smelter](Smelter.md) (returning 1/2 bar/block, a 50% return\*).

> (\* This can be especially useful if you can acquire desired metal blocks via [trade](Trade.md), though in multiples of 2 *and* then melt both at the same furnace, to produce a full bar of that metal.)

### Blocks vs. rocks

The clearest distinction between a [building](Building.md) built with [raw stone](Stone.md) and a building built with a stone block is that more buildings can be built of blocks, as each stone produces 4 blocks. Note that this does not apply to [glass](Glass.md), or [ceramic](Ceramic.md) blocks/bricks, which are only made in sets of 1; [metal](Metal.md) blocks are also made in sets of 1, but since you get 4 bars at a time from smelting ore, the end result is equivalent to stone.

Blocks have less volume, and thus [weigh](Weight.md) much less than raw forms of construction materials - stone blocks weigh only 6% as much as a raw stone, and wooden blocks weigh 12% as much as the log from which they are carved. This weight difference can dramatically reduce hauling time, especially if you are using large amounts of materials. Forming metal bars into blocks makes no difference in regards to weight.

Building a bridge out of blocks instead of rocks also **cuts the building time by two thirds**, independent of clearing and hauling time. This difference in building time presumably extends to other buildings and constructions where you can choose between blocks and rocks. The appearance of [bridges](Bridge.md) and paved [roads](Road.md) will be different depending on whether blocks or rocks are used. The use of blocks is indicated by the floor tile `+`, and the use of rocks is indicated by a rough ground tile `≈`.

Unlike raw stone or wood, up to 30 blocks can be stored in each [bin](Bin.md) in bar/block [stockpiles](Stockpile.md), which can improve hauling time between stockpiles. A [minecart](Minecart.md) can carry up to 83 blocks.

Blocks are more valuable than rocks, having a [base value](Value.md#items-with-material-but-without-quality) of 5, compared to the raw material value of 3 for stone, wood or glass, while metal bars already have a base value of 5. This, combined with the yield of 4 blocks per stone, nets a value increase of 17 when using basic stones for blocks.

Metal ores and economic stone will still produce multiple stone blocks if cut in a mason's workshop. However, this breaks use for any purpose other than building and [strange moods](Strange_mood.md), such as [reactions](Reaction.md): blocks of [flux](Flux~2.md) **cannot** be used for making [pig iron](Pig_iron~2.md) or [steel](Steel.md), nor can blocks of [ore](Ore.md) be [smelted](Smelter.md) into metal (without modding reactions). Note that "filler" blocks of [raw adamantine](Raw_adamantine.md) are excellent for increasing [artifact](Artifact.md) value without wasting too much adamantine on legendary doors, tables, etc.
