> *For the month, see [Calendar](Calendar.md).*

**Limestone**, like [dolomite](Dolomite.md) and [chalk](Chalk.md), is a [flux](Flux~2.md) [stone](Stone.md) which forms entire [sedimentary layers](Sedimentary_layer.md). In addition, limestone layers are the only sedimentary layers to contain [galena](Galena.md) or [malachite](Malachite.md).

Limestone chunks can be used to make: [steel](Steel.md) [bars](Bar.md), [pig iron](Pig_iron~2.md) [bars](Bar.md), [quicklime](Quicklime.md), [furniture](Furniture.md) and can be used in building

In general, limestone layers tend to yield more [ores](Ore.md) and [gems](Gem.md) per volume mined than those of other stones, which makes them a prime candidate for exploratory [mining](Mining.md). Any overburden thus generated will also be doubly valuable compared to regular stone for [crafting](Craft.md) as a result of limestone's flux property.

"Limestone" is also the name of the seventh month of the dwarven [calendar](Calendar.md), covering early Autumn.

### In real life

Limestone is a sedimentary rock composed largely of minerals like [calcite](Calcite.md). It can also contain silica in the form of [chert](Chert.md), and varying amounts of [clay](Clay.md), [silt](Silt.md) and [sand](Sand.md) carried in by [rivers](River.md). In addition to its real-world use in steel production, limestone is highly durable and weather-resistant, and has been used in construction for, effectively, all of human history -- the Great Pyramids are built of limestone blocks.
