# Animal person

[![Animal people preview2.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/1/10/Animal_people_preview2.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Animal_people_preview2.png)

**Animal people** are half-man, half-beast [creatures](Creature.md) with various animal-like traits, some beneficial (such as [flying](Flying.md), and [poisons](Syndrome.md)), or detrimental ([thievery](Steals_items.md)), inherited from their base animal. They generally have significantly longer [lifespans](Age.md) than the base animals, as well as the ability to [learn](Learns.md). They can be legless, but get arms even if their base animal is limbless. Their body size is the average of their base animal's size and 70,000 (the size of a [human](Human.md)), working out so that the smallest animal people are 35,000cm³, half the size of a [human](Human.md), but they can get [very large](Sperm_whale_man.md). Most animal people can join [civilizations](Civilization.md) and live in [sites](Site.md), or come to your fort as [visitors](Visitor.md).

Animal people are playable creatures in [adventurer mode](Adventurer_mode.md). If they became established members of a playable civ ([dwarf](Dwarf.md), [elf](Elf.md), human), they will appear listed in the creature selection screen alongside the main races. Otherwise, they are selectable under Intelligent Wilderness Creature. Some animal people may not be available due to having [died out](Extinction.md) or being uncontacted (e.g. excluded to an isolated island).

Wild animal people wander on the surface in [savage](Savage.md) environments or dwell underground in the [caverns](Cavern.md); these two populations form distinct groups:

- Savage animal people behave mostly like common animals, wandering the wilderness and occasionally joining human, dwarven or elven society during [world generation](World_generation.md). When encountered in fortress mode gameplay, they are essentially animals who can't be [butchered](Butcher.md), incapable of any sort of non-violent interaction. In adventure mode gameplay, you can speak with them and possibly recruit them as followers - they may be neutral or, somewhat more commonly, hostile, though with rare exceptions capable of speech. Like their underground counterparts, they tend to have [spears](Spear.md) and [blowguns](Blowgun.md), though they may also be unarmed.
- Subterranean animal people are a civilization in themselves, rarely encountered, in small groups or "tribes". They are equipped with primitive weaponry such as wooden [spears](Spear.md) and [blowguns](Blowgun.md) (they are the only known source of [blowdarts](Blowdart.md)). Subterranean animal people will be labelled as Hostile upon discovery. Despite this, they are non-aggressive and will generally ignore your dwarves unless provoked. Encountering them often comes as a surprise, since there is no way to know about their presence before embarking. They do not appear in the [![Ui Y.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/1/19/Ui_Y.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Ui_Y.png)Y civilizations [menu](Menu.md).

Subterranean animal people are also found in generated [sewers](Sewer.md), formed from collections of outcasts. Graphically, animal people are given their own sprites. Unlike dwarves, humans, elves, [goblins](Goblin.md) and [kobolds](Kobold.md), where each of the body parts of the creature are assembled from separate smaller pieces to combine into one sprite, based on their physical traits and items in their possession, animal people will use the same exact sprite regardless of any differing qualities. Like almost every other creature in the game, animal people will have one standard sprite, and one [undead](Undead.md) sprite. Unlike most "man" creatures, [Amphibian Man](Amphibian_man.md), [Ant Man](Ant_man.md), [Plump Helmet Man](Plump_helmet_man.md), [Reptile Man](Reptile_man.md), [Rodent Man](Rodent_man.md), and [Serpent Man](Serpent_man.md) do not `[COPY_TAGS_FROM]` their base species. Animal people will not inherit the `[MEANDERER]` tag from their animal counterparts.

#### Ethics

Being an entity in their own right, subterranean animal people possess ethics, much like other civs. Those particular ethics are incomplete for now, being directly copied from kobolds and their cultural [values](Personality_value.md) all set to zero (neutral).

Like kobolds, subterranean animal people consider the killing of other living beings acceptable, but treason is unthinkable, and they'll respond to the murder of their own kind by exiling the offender from their tribe. They oppose torturing for information and the torture of animals, but consider torturing for fun appropriate. Assault is considered a personal matter, while slavery, the taking of animal trophies, and the consumption of sapient beings (which includes other animal people) are all unthinkable acts.

### Body structure

While the standard description, *"A person with the head of a..."*, suggests a [human](Human.md)-like body like a [minotaur](Minotaur.md) has, animal people use the same tissue types as their base animal. For example, insect people have a [chitin](Chitin.md) exoskeleton, while mammalian people are covered in [fur](Hair.md).

If the base creature does not normally have arms, then they get a single, three-fingered pair of arms by default. Insect bodies have four arms, and arachnids get six - [mantis men](Mantis_man.md) are an exception, only having two ([deadly](Natural_weapon.md)) arms, as a [mantis](Mantis.md) uses `[BODY:INSECT_4LEGS_2ARMS]` instead of `[BODY:INSECT]`. Bird men receive a pair of four-fingered arms in addition to their wings, whereas [bat men](Bat_man.md) have a single pair of arm-wings. Unlike other arthropods, crustacean people only have two arms, with no fingers.

Most, but not all, animal people have a pair of legs. Creatures with [hooves](Horn.md) or flippers will use those for [locomotion](Gait.md), while species without toes do not gain any. A rule of thumb is that creatures that end in a tail remain legless as animal people. Due to [graphical](Graphics.md) [oversights](Bug.md), the sprite for some creatures (such as the [snail man](Snail_man.md)) may not match their body plan.

Animal people keep any additional [body parts](Body_parts.md) their species may have, including the head-arms of cephalopod people and the pincers of a [bark scorpion man](Bark_scorpion_man.md), as well as tails, [shells](Shell.md), [horns](Horn.md), or internal organs.

### History

Present from the first official release of the game, the animal people did not exist as a class but were instead part of a small group of subterranean creatures that harassed your dwarves. [Frogmen](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/23a:Frogman), [lizardmen](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/23a:Lizardman), and [snakemen](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/23a:Snakeman) emerged from [cave rivers](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/23a:Cave_river), and [antmen](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/23a:Antman), [batmen](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/23a:Batman), and [ratmen](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/23a:Ratman) would come out from [chasms](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/23a:Chasm) to attack.

[v0.27.169.32a](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/40d:Release_information) released three-dimensional worlds, and subterranean creatures were moved to flock near generated [chasms](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/40d:Chasm), [underground rivers](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/40d:Underground_river), and [underground pools](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/40d:Underground_pool). [Cave swallowmen](Cave_swallow_man.md) and [olmmen](Olm_man.md) were introduced. The first set of above-ground savage animal people were also introduced: [leechmen](Leech_man.md), [snailmen](Snail_man.md), [slugmen](Slug_man.md), and [tigermen](Tiger_man.md).

In [v0.31](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/v0.31:Release_information), every creature was renamed and/or had their properties revamped (for example, "frogman" was changed to "amphibian man" and "batman" was changed to "bat man"). Not all animal people were redefined in this version, and some continued to use old codes for many releases. The version also introduced "[creature variation](Creature_variation_token.md)" templates, which made converting existing animals to humanoid (and giant) variations much more efficient. Underground features were expanded to the current [caverns](Cavern.md), and subterranean animal people formed civilizations. More anthropomorphic variations of animals present at the time were added.

More animal people were added to the game in [v0.34](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/v0.34:Release_information).

[v0.42.01](Release_information/0.42.01.md) allowed animal people to join civilizations, live in sites, and visit forts. Animal people became playable as adventurers. (A grizzly bear hammerman, for example, has much to offer a dwarven society.) [v0.42.04](Release_information/0.42.04.md) added more animal people to the game.
