An **office** (called a study in some game menus) is a [zone](Zone.md) required by certain [nobles](Nobles.md) and administrators. Some higher-ranking nobles require a "throne room" - the same thing, just fancier. You can simply designate an office from the zone menu. An office requires a [throne](Throne.md) or [chair](Chair.md) and does not (necessarily) have to be a separate enclosed space separated from other areas by walls and a [door](Door.md).

Note that if you assign an office to a dwarf, sometimes they will eat a meal in their office, and complain about the lack of [tables](Table.md) - to avoid this thought, put a table next to the chair in said office.

Depending on the [zone value](Zone.md#quality-and-value), offices get a rank:

### Reducing Office Space

A "meager office" can consist of a 1-tile-diameter area designated from a single chair as a study/office, and assigned to a particular dwarf ([![UI add worker.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/thumb/9/94/UI_add_worker.png/28px-UI_add_worker.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:UI_add_worker.png)). You will probably first encounter this need in the [bookkeeper](Bookkeeper~2.md) or the [manager](Manager.md) - assigning one of the chairs in the dining room for them is perfectly acceptable.

If you put a table next to the office chair and designate it as a dining room as well, both rooms will be used like they are supposed to - however, overlapping rooms drop considerably in value rating, making it harder to meet demands by higher-ranking nobles. While this can be countered with high-value [furniture](Furniture.md) (artifacts are very helpful), actual individual areas, designated separately from other areas (whether or not separated by walls) are the easier way to meet demands.
