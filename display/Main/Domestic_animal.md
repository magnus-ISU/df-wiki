**Domestic animals** are [creatures](Creature.md) that can be used by a [civilization](Civilization.md) one way or another without any [training](Animal_training~2.md) required, usually for security or food. Distinguished by having the `[COMMON_DOMESTIC]` tag in the [raws](Raw_file.md), they can be bought at [embark](Embark.md) or [traded](Trade.md) for with [caravans](Caravan.md). Wild versions of domesticated animals ([horses](Horse.md), [turkeys](Turkey.md), etc.) can be captured in [cage](Cage.md) [traps](Trap.md) and receive training, which will turn them completely tame afterwards.

[Dwarf](Dwarf.md), [human](Human.md) and [elf](Elf.md) civilizations have access to the same variety of domestic animals, which are listed below. [Goblins](Goblin.md) can't use these, but rather have access to domesticated [beak dogs](Beak_dog.md), which dwarves can only access by capturing in the wild or as [spoils of war](Mission.md). Meanwhile, [kobolds](Kobold.md) and subterranean [animal people](Animal_people.md) have no 'domestic' animals at all.

Every embark starts with two free, randomly-selected domestic **draft animals** which arrive with the [wagon](Wagon_(embark).md) at the fortress site. These can be any of the animals in the list below which are available at embark that have the `[WAGON_PULLER]` tag *(i.e. [horses](Horse.md), [yaks](Yak.md) and [water buffaloes](Water_buffalo.md))*. Note that the two animals do not (intentionally) come as a "matched pair", and so may not be from the same species. Embarking with a sufficient quantity of goods can result in multiple wagons, each of which comes with another pair of randomly selected animals. Since fortresses cannot move their wagons *after* embark, these "draft" animals can be [slaughtered](Butcher.md) or kept for breeding, or can become [pets](Pet.md) for making certain dwarves [happy](Preferences.md).

When purchasing\* draft animals at embark, the player may choose which gender of animals (only one male is necessary for breeding). [Depending on the animal](Cat.md), you may wish to limit breeding altogether, or assign a [gelder](Gelder.md).

> *(\* Note that the two free draft animals per wagon are not "purchased", and so are randomly generated, both re species and gender.)*

### Comparison of domestic mammals

When butchered, all adult domestic mammals yield one [skull](Skull.md) and one [raw hide](Leather.md). All milkable mammals give the same value of [milk](Milk.md) and appear to be milkable at the same frequency. The embark costs shown apply to both adults and juveniles of either sex. All domestic mammals reach adolescence at 1 year and adulthood at 2 years, except [cavies](Cavy.md), which take half as long. All [grazing](Grazer.md) animals require a [pasture](Pasture.md); the lower the 'grazer' value, the larger the size of the required pasture. Animals can give birth to 1-3 young at a time (unless they use the `[LITTERSIZE]` token to adjust the minimum and maximum), though those with the `[MULTIPLE_LITTER_RARE]` token only have a 1/500 chance of giving birth to more than one.

### Comparison of domestic poultry

All domestic poultry have a [pet](Pet.md) value of 10 and cost 6[☼](Currency.md) at embark. Blue peafowl, geese, and guineafowl reach adolescence at six months and are full-grown after one year; ducks, chickens and turkeys reach adolescence at one year and are full-grown after two years. When butchered, all full-grown poultry yield one [skull](Skull.md); those that yield bones also yield a [raw hide](Leather.md). However, domestic poultry are much more efficient when used for [egg production](Egg_production.md) than as a meat source; over the lifetime of an average hen, many more meals can be produced from the eggs. This is especially true for ducks and guineafowl.
