[![Wealth preview.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/9/9d/Wealth_preview.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Wealth_preview.png)

Your **created wealth** is the sum product of all the labors of your fortress. It is visible on the [wealth tooltip](Status.md#fortress-name-and-status)\* once you have a [broker](Broker.md) with the [appraisal](Appraisal.md) [skill](Skill.md) and a [bookkeeper](Bookkeeper~2.md) maintaining your records, and will update with the continual maintenance of your [stocks](Stocks.md) by the bookkeeper.

> *(\* On the left of the bar at the top of your screen is your basic fortress info, its name and size. Mouse-hovering over this area reveals the wealth tooltip, a basic breakdown of the fortress' wealth.)*

Wealth is the sum of all objects of [value](Item_value.md) in your fortress, including raw materials like [stones](Stone.md) and [metal bars](Metal_bar.md), [buildings](Building.md), engravings, and every kind of created good, all of which contribute to your total wealth to various degrees. [Corpses](Corpse.md) (*other than certain [bones](Bone.md), as seen below*) and [remains](Remains.md) of creatures have no wealth value.

[Artifact](Artifact.md) items are usually one of the largest influences on fortress wealth, especially artifacts made out of or [decorated](Decorate.md) using valuable materials.

The display of your wealth on the main status screen is broken up into several categories: [weapons](Weapon.md), [armor](Armor.md) and [garb](Clothing.md), [furniture](Furniture.md), other objects (like [finished goods](Finished_goods~2.md)), architecture ([buildings](Building.md), [constructions](Constructions.md), [engravings](Engravings.md) and such), [displayed](Display_furniture.md), and held/worn (items created internally that have been claimed by dwarves, like clothing). Also the displayed total created wealth is always the sum of the aforementioned categories, but if for any category the shown (wealth) number has either a tilde as a prefix (which means that the value is only an estimate) or a question mark as a suffix (which means that the value is actually only a guess and can be way off mark), then the calculated value for that category is off (and the brokers appraisal skill level is to low to give a better or precise estimate), and thus the total created wealth value will also be wrong (allthough that number will never have a tilde as a prefix or question mark as a suffix).

See [accounts](Account.md) for the more abstract wealth count, used in [world generation](World_generation.md) to determine building ownership and help ground various acts of corruption and [villainy](Villain.md).

### Imports and exports

[![](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/c/c3/Wealth-tooltip.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Wealth-tooltip.png)

[https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Wealth-tooltip.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Wealth-tooltip.png)

[Wealth tooltip](Status.md#fortress-name-and-status) in Premium. Accuracy is based on your current [Bookkeeping](Bookkeeper~2.md).

Items made off-site are not counted in your total fortress wealth, and are instead listed as imports. This only applies so long as the object is unchanged; a decorated imported object will be made your own, and its value will be moved from imports to wealth. This is important when trading with caravans, as they will not accept goods stolen or lost by a previous caravan of that civilization. It is listed under "Imported Wealth".

Similarly, items made in the fortress that leave the map after being traded to a [caravan](Caravan.md) are counted as exports, listed under "Exported Wealth."

### Influences

Wealth influences various game features. Having more fortress wealth increases the amount of [migrants](Migrant.md) you get per wave. Assuming that you are equipped to handle the new dwarves, this is usually a good thing. However, having an *extreme* surplus of wealth can cause your fortress to grow so quickly that the migrant arrivals outpace your ability to house and feed them or give them useful things to do. Drowning in migrants is a very real danger; make sure you are always equipped with surplus food, drink, beds and work stations.

A certain level of exports and overall wealth is required to acquire economically-linked [holdings](Holding.md). However, for smaller sites, fortress wealth is the more important factor in determining economic linkage. Also, having a certain amount of fortress wealth and exported wealth is required to ascend the [noble](Noble.md) ladder: a [baron](Baron.md) requires 100,000 units of wealth overall and 10,000 wealth worth of exports. These values increase to 200,000 in overall wealth and 20,000 in exports for a [count](Count.md), and 300,000 units of wealth with 30,000 in exports for a [duke](Duke.md). The [monarch](Monarch.md) has some fairly complicated requirements based on a few different categories.

On the negative side, having more wealth attracts more frequent and larger attacks from enemies. At first, this will be an above-average amount of [thieves](Thief.md), but as the game progresses and your wealth continues to grow, this will develop into [ambushes](Ambush.md) or [sieges](Siege.md) from opposing factions and visits from [megabeasts](Megabeast.md). This keeps the game from being boring, but too much [fun](Fun.md) is also a bad thing; if you have a hard time dealing with the numerous waves of immigrants, you're probably not equipped to deal with a full-on siege.

### Building and limiting wealth

Building wealth is simple -- just commit more people to useful industries and continue growing. You will want to establish a major [industry](Industry.md) and commit a number of skilled dwarves to it, allowing you to spend grand sums on caravans and get everything you absolutely need quickly and painlessly. If you intend to go this route, be sure to create plenty of extra [bins](Bin.md) to ease the transfer of items to the trade depot and prevent stockpiles from becoming too full.

When first starting out, it's typically best to not focus on building fortress wealth too much until you have an effective military that can deal with the [fun](Megabeast.md) [things](Werebeasts.md) that such wealth can cause. However, in your first year, you probably want to create goods to be able to [trade](Trade.md) with the autumn dwarven [caravan](Caravan.md), in order to obtain goods that are not available at your location, or that your fortress cannot produce on its own yet. A simple way to create a decent amount of wealth without much effort is to [cook](Cook.md) a surplus of [lavish meals](Lavish_meal.md). Be cautious about selling prepared meals to the [elves](Elf.md) though, as food with meat-based ingredients, and the wooden barrels in which the meals are likely stored, are [unacceptable](Trading.md#unacceptable-items) to them.

[Metals](Metal.md) are great materials for creating wealth. In particular, many locations have an [ore](Ore.md) of [iron](Iron.md) or [silver](Silver.md) - both are of moderate value and can be made into [weapons](Weapon.md), [furniture](Furniture.md) or crafts, while [statues](Statue.md) make for high-value furniture. Certain butchered [animals](Animal.md) have high-value bones or skulls. [Ceramics](Ceramic.md), especially [stoneware](Stoneware.md) made from [fire clay](Fire_clay.md) can provide a continuous source of decent valued goods. Rough [gems](Gem.md) can be hard to find, but a highly skilled [gem setter](Gem_setter.md) can decorate objects to add considerable value to furniture or crafts.

On the opposite side, there are the fortresses that would much rather establish a baseline of sorts before embarking on an expansionist binge - getting a full defensive militia or defensive structures up, for instance, or penetrating an [aquifer](Aquifer.md) without having to waste precious reserves on more dwarves and more enemies. These players concentrate on low-value activities like carpentry, masonry, and mining, and only produce enough trade goods to get what is necessary from the caravans. Although they are slower to grow, they also afford their players more time to plan and to lay the groundwork for the future of the fortress.

Note that, no matter what, you *will* have to deal with *some* growth -- besides the natural expansion of your fortress, there's also the issue of [artifacts](Artifact.md). Do what you will, but every once in a while a dwarf will [claim a workshop](Strange_mood.md) and produce a valuable trinket, and all you can hope for is that it's not worth too much. It's worth noting that artifact furniture is counted three separate times toward created wealth: as furniture, as architecture, and as displayed. You may wish to hold off on installing that legendary [coffer](Coffer.md) that's worth three times as much as your fortress when just sitting in a stockpile.

Most players choose to walk the middle line, getting together the necessary industries, but concentrating on the [metal industry](Metal_industry~2.md) early on, to get together their arms and armor. Although [steel](Steel.md) is worth its weight in [gold](Gold.md), it is much more useful deflecting goblin arrows from your fortress defenders than as a [statue](Statue.md) decorating your [lobby](Meeting_area.md).
