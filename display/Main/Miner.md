**Miner** is the skill associated with both the **mining** [labor](Labor.md) and its associated profession. Miners are necessary to build any kind of [fortress](Fortress.md) that is [underground](Underground.md) or involves [stone](Stone.md). They are required to dig tunnels, [channels](Channel.md), [stairs](Stair.md) and [ramps](Ramp.md), as well as remove said stairs and ramps. Miners require a [pick](Pick.md) to do any work.

Mining speed is controlled by the experience of the miner, and (as of [53.07](Release_information/53.07.md)) the [material](Material.md) and [quality](Quality.md) of the pick. Note that miners do not automatically exchange their picks for better ones. To "fix" this, disable the mining labor for a dwarf and let them drop their old pick. Then re-enable the labor, and they will seek out the best pick available.

Miners gain experience for every tile mined, which means they gain experience faster in easier-to-dig soil and slower in stone. Due to the constant demand, straightforward work and fast experience curve, miners level-up to [legendary skill](Skill.md) faster than any other profession. Most players will have one or two legendary miners within thirty minutes of gameplay.

Mining skill affects only the speed at which stone is mined, not the chance of acquiring resources from mining. Normal rocks and ore deposits drop an average of 1 boulder for every 4 squares mined; in compensation, one ore gives 4 bars of [metal](Metal.md) (although some types of ore sometimes give 1-4 extra bars of another metal) and one stone boulder yields 4 [blocks](Block.md). Gem clusters drop rough [gems](Gem.md) 100% of the time. Graphically, miners will often wear light grey tunics to resemble their occupation.

In order for any mining to be done, the [![Ui m.png](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/images/d/d7/Ui_m.png)](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/File:Ui_m.png)mining [designation](Designations_menu~2.md) must be used.

### Mining behavior

> *Main article: [Mining](Mining.md)*

If a large area is designated for mining (with the same mining priority), miners will start clearing out the area starting from the accessible designated mining area which is nearest to them (when they decide to mine, eg. take the first mining spot, but, if the dwarf does not have a pickaxe in inventory, the decision where to mine is done after picking up a pickaxe), here nearest means whichever location is nearest to them in the maximum norm (actual pathing distance to get to the spot is irrelevant).

Once mining, a dwarf will automatically continue on to mine any connected tiles (on the same level) designated for mining, even diagonally, rather than shift to mine a discontinuous tile. But once all such tiles (which were designated for mining) have been mined the dwarf will pick any tile designated for mining (which is not already tagged by another miner as the next mining spot) with the minimum distance (in the maximum norm) from the position the miner is currently at, which might just be a tile one z-level above or below the current dwarf's position (even if the actual pathing distance is much longer than the actual pathing distance to the next tile, designated for mining, on the current z-level). If more than one tile has the same minimum distance than it is more or less random, which tile is picked next.

### Mining speed

Prior to version 53.07, a zero-skill Miner took 20 job ticks (5 per 'wear' on the wall being dug - a soil tile would just be 5) at 10 frames per tick (depending on the miner's attributes), for a total of about 200 frames to mine a single tile. A Proficient miner, however, did the same work in only 60% of the time (12 job ticks, 3 per wear level). A Legendary miner did the job in 20% of the time (4 job ticks, 1 per wear level). If you're doing a lot of digging, [starting](Embark.md) with a semi-skilled Miner can save you a lot of time.

As of version 53.07, however, formula is significantly different:

1. Start with a base job duration of 1200 (per wear level - stone does this 4 times, while soil only does it once)
2. Multiply the duration by `(30 - SKILL_LEVEL) / 30` (with SKILL_LEVEL being the unit's [adjusted](Skill.md#skill-penalties) mining skill level, capped at 15)
3. Multiply the duration by `100 / FORT_DIG_SPEED` or `100 / INVADER_DIG_SPEED` (depending on the miner's allegiance)
4. Take the `[SHEAR_FRACTURE]` values for both the stone being mined and the pick being used
5. Calculate `PICK_STRENGTH = (PICK_SHEAR_FRACTURE * 10 * (PICK_QUALITY + 5) / 5)`; if PICK_STRENGTH is greater than STONE_FRACTURE, then multiply by the duration by `STONE_FRACTURE / PICK_STRENGTH`

As a result, using a stronger pick *significantly* increases the speed at which tiles can be mined - nearly all vanilla stone types have a SHEAR_FRACTURE value of 15000 (with the sole exceptions being [obsidian](Obsidian.md) at 35000 and [raw adamantine](Raw_adamantine.md) at 3,000,000), so a basic [copper](Copper.md) pick can mine them in 32 job ticks and an iron pick takes just 20 job ticks.

### Combat

The miner skill is also used when fighting with a pickaxe. Pickaxes are brutally effective [weapons](Weapon.md), combining a piercing and a slashing attack, and make really good weapons for adventurers. [Immigrants](Immigrants.md) can come to the fort with low-quality [copper](Copper.md) or [bronze](Bronze.md) pickaxes, so consider replacing them with better-quality materials, like [iron](Iron.md) made by skilled weaponsmiths. Despite their effectiveness, however, a miner who is attacked when they're busy mining will not generally use their pick in combat, unless they are forced to.
