A **wetland** is a type of [biome](Biome.md), flooded permanently or only periodically, with an abundance of [foliage](Plants.md), including heavy, specialized [tree](Tree.md)-growth and a vast ecosystem. The landscape is very flat, with only occasional, small gradients. [Ponds](Pond.md) are abundant. Wetlands tend to form around [oceanic](Ocean.md) coastlines, especially where [rivers](River.md) meet the ocean.

They generally have a high [water](Water.md) table, meaning that an accessible [aquifer](Aquifer.md) is only a few tiles below the surface (and in occasionally unlucky circumstances, directly beneath the surface). It can be incredibly difficult to nigh-impossible to extract [stones](Stone.md) from these areas, making the long-term feasibility of a fort doubtful; it is recommended that an additional, non-saturated biome be selected as well. The stratification is often hard to ascertain, as it is difficult to penetrate the aquifer. Usually there are a few layers of [clay](Clay.md) and [loam](Loam.md) followed, eventually, by stone and rock beneath.

Wetlands with [salt water](Water.md#salt-water) make habitability more precarious, as the aquifers and surrounding ponds are not drinkable. The water first has to be [desalinated](Water.md#salt-water).

The two main categories of wetlands are **marshes** and **swamps**. The types of wetlands are:

- Temperate freshwater marsh
- Temperate saltwater marsh
- Tropical freshwater marsh
- Tropical saltwater marsh
- Temperate freshwater swamp
- Temperate saltwater swamp
- Tropical freshwater swamp
- Tropical saltwater swamp
- [Mangrove](Mangrove.md) swamp (a variant of tropical saltwater swamps)
